Energy storage is essential for integrating variable renewable sources such as wind and solar. Lithium-ion batteries now dominate new grid storage. Global battery storage capacity additions reached about 42 gigawatts in 2023, nearly double the roughly 22 gigawatts added in 2022, according to BloombergNEF and the International Energy Agency. China, the United States and Europe accounted for more than 90 percent of installations. By the end of 2023 the world had roughly 90 gigawatts of grid-scale battery storage in operation.

Battery prices have fallen sharply. BloombergNEF's annual survey found that the average price of a lithium-ion battery pack was 139 dollars per kilowatt-hour in 2023, down 14 percent from 2022 and about 90 percent lower than in 2013. In 2024 the average fell further to 115 dollars per kilowatt-hour. Turnkey grid storage systems cost around 250 to 300 dollars per kilowatt-hour in 2023. Lithium iron phosphate chemistry, known as LFP, has taken a large share of the stationary market because it is cheaper and safer than nickel-based chemistries, and it supports 6,000 or more charge cycles.

California is a leader in utility batteries. The state had more than 10 gigawatts of battery storage by the end of 2023, up from just 770 megawatts in 2019, and the batteries regularly supply more than 20 percent of evening peak demand in spring and summer. The Moss Landing Energy Storage Facility in Monterey County is the world's largest battery installation, with a capacity of 750 megawatts and 3,000 megawatt-hours after its 2023 expansion. A fire at its older Vistra building in January 2025 drew attention to safety concerns. The Hornsdale Power Reserve in South Australia, built by Tesla in 2017 with an initial capacity of 100 megawatts and later expanded to 150 megawatts, helped lower frequency control prices by about 90 percent in the region.

Other technologies are emerging for longer durations. Pumped hydro storage still provides the vast majority of storage capacity worldwide, at about 160 gigawatts. Sodium-ion batteries entered commercial production in China in 2023, with energy densities of roughly 140 watt-hours per kilogram. Iron-air batteries from Form Energy promise 100 hours of discharge at a target cost of 20 dollars per kilowatt-hour, with a first commercial deployment in Minnesota expected from 2025. Green hydrogen, compressed air and thermal storage are being tested for seasonal storage, but their round-trip efficiencies are lower: electrolysis-based hydrogen storage typically returns only 30 to 40 percent of the electricity put in, compared with 85 to 95 percent for lithium-ion batteries.
