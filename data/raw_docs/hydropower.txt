Hydropower is the oldest and largest source of renewable electricity in the world. In 2023 it generated about 4,200 terawatt-hours, roughly 14 percent of global electricity, according to the International Energy Agency. Installed hydropower capacity, including pumped storage, was about 1,416 gigawatts at the end of 2023. Conventional hydropower accounts for roughly 1,270 gigawatts and pumped storage for about 180 gigawatts, so the totals overlap slightly in different statistics.

The largest hydropower station in the world is the Three Gorges Dam on the Yangtze River in China. Construction began in 1994 and the dam was fully commissioned in 2012 with an installed capacity of 22,500 megawatts from 32 main turbines. It typically generates between 85 and 100 terawatt-hours per year, and in 2020 it set a record of 111.8 terawatt-hours. The second largest is the Baihetan Dam, also in China, completed in 2022 with a capacity of 16,000 megawatts. The Itaipu Dam on the border of Brazil and Paraguay has a capacity of 14,000 megawatts and supplies about 90 percent of the electricity used in Paraguay.

China is the largest hydropower producer with roughly 420 gigawatts of capacity, followed by Brazil with about 110 gigawatts, the United States with about 80 gigawatts and Canada with about 82 gigawatts. Norway generates around 88 percent of its electricity from hydropower, and Paraguay and Ethiopia produce almost all of their power from water. The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam on the Blue Nile, with a capacity of 5,150 megawatts, started generating electricity in 2022.

Hydropower plants are very efficient, converting up to 90 percent of the energy in flowing water into electricity, and a well-maintained dam can operate for 50 to 100 years. Reservoirs also provide flood control, irrigation and drinking water. Pumped storage hydropower works as a giant battery and accounts for more than 90 percent of the world's grid-scale energy storage capacity.

There are environmental and social costs. Large reservoirs flood land and displace communities; the Three Gorges project displaced about 1.3 million people. Dams alter river ecosystems and block fish migration, and reservoirs in tropical regions can emit methane. Droughts also reduce output: in 2023 low rainfall in China, Mexico and the western United States cut hydropower generation, and the global growth of hydropower was only about 0.2 percent that year. New capacity additions were about 20 gigawatts in 2023, much less than solar or wind.
