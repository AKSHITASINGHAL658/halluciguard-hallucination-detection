Policy and investment have driven the rapid expansion of renewable energy. According to BloombergNEF, global investment in the energy transition reached 1.77 trillion dollars in 2023, a 17 percent increase over 2022, with renewable energy alone attracting about 623 billion dollars. The International Energy Agency reported that global clean energy investment, including grids, storage and electric vehicles, was about 1.8 trillion dollars in 2023, compared with around 1.0 trillion dollars spent on fossil fuels.

In December 2023, at the COP28 climate summit in Dubai, nearly 200 countries agreed to a global goal of tripling renewable energy capacity to at least 11,000 gigawatts by 2030. The International Renewable Energy Agency estimates that the world added a record 473 gigawatts of renewable capacity in 2023, an increase of about 14 percent over 2022 and enough to bring the total to 3,870 gigawatts. Renewables accounted for 86 percent of all new power capacity added that year. To meet the tripling target, annual additions would need to grow by roughly 16.4 percent every year until 2030.

The European Union's Renewable Energy Directive, revised in 2023, raised the binding EU target for renewables to 42.5 percent of final energy consumption by 2030, with an aspiration of 45 percent. In 2023 renewables supplied about 44 percent of electricity in the European Union, and wind and solar together generated more electricity than natural gas for the first time. Germany passed a law targeting 80 percent renewable electricity by 2030, and its renewable share of net public electricity reached about 56 percent in 2023.

In the United States, the Inflation Reduction Act, signed on 16 August 2022, authorized roughly 369 billion dollars for energy security and climate programs over ten years. It extended the investment tax credit and production tax credit, set at 30 percent for projects meeting wage and apprenticeship requirements, and introduced a 45X manufacturing credit for domestic production of solar modules and batteries. Following the law, companies announced more than 100 billion dollars in new clean energy manufacturing investments within two years.

China remains the dominant investor, accounting for about 38 percent of global energy transition investment in 2023 and for more than 80 percent of the world's manufacturing capacity for solar modules and polysilicon. India has set a target of 500 gigawatts of non-fossil electricity capacity by 2030 and had installed about 190 gigawatts by the end of 2023. Developing economies face higher financing costs, and the cost of capital in many emerging markets is two to three times that in advanced economies, which slows deployment despite excellent solar and wind resources.
